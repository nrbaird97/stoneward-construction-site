American Fork, Utah County

Backyard ADUs are now allowed in American Fork.

American Fork adopted detached ADU rules on September 22, 2026, to meet Utah's new SB 284. If you own a lot of 11,000 square feet or more, a factory-built home in the backyard may now be an option. Stoneward is based right here in American Fork.

About American Fork

A canyon-mouth city with a long history of backyard sheds.

American Fork sits at the north end of Utah Valley, where American Fork Canyon opens toward Mount Timpanogos and the city runs west toward Utah Lake. Lehi is to the north, Highland to the east and Pleasant Grove to the south. The historic Main Street and the Steel Days celebration give the city a settled, small-town feel, and many of its neighborhoods are built on roomy lots that have held a shop or a detached garage for decades.

Until this fall the city allowed an apartment inside the house but not a separate unit in the yard. That is why the September 22 vote matters here more than in a city that already allowed detached ADUs: a lot of American Fork homeowners are seeing this option for the first time, and the lots that clear 11,000 square feet are common in the older parts of town.

What it means for you

What this means for a American Fork homeowner.

Family close by

A parent, an adult child or a caregiver gets their own front door and kitchen, a few steps from yours.

Long-term rental income

Rentals of 90 days or more are allowed, and you must live on the property. Run the numbers before you commit, and we will help.

The 25 percent rule

The ADU and every other accessory building together can cover at most a quarter of the rear yard, so a compact footprint is what makes a crowded yard work.

Short disruption

A factory-built unit is built off site and set by crane, so your yard is a work zone for weeks, not many months.

What American Fork will check before it says yes.

The city requires adequate utility service and capacity and routes each project through its Development Review Committee. At the September 22 meeting a council member asked how added households would affect water and sewer in older neighborhoods and whether impact fees would cover it, so ask early which lines serve your lot and what fees apply. We trace sewer, water and power to your yard and price the trenching line by line before you pick a plan.

Getting it built in American Fork

From a American Fork lot to move-in.

The full process
  1. Lot review. We confirm 11,000 square feet, locate the setbacks and measure the rear yard against the 25 percent limit.
  2. Choose a plan. Under 650 square feet means one parking space. Pick the plan, then one of four finish series.
  3. Development Review Committee. We help you assemble the site plan and plan set American Fork's committee will review, then the building permit.
  4. Foundation and utilities. Site work happens in your yard while the home is built in the factory.
  5. Set day and finish. A crane sets the home, then we join the seams, connect utilities and walk it with you.

Questions

American Fork ADU questions, answered.

Can I build a detached ADU in American Fork?

Yes, if your lot qualifies. The City Council adopted detached ADU rules on September 22, 2026, ahead of the October 1 state deadline. The lot must be at least 11,000 square feet with an existing detached single-family home on it. American Fork already allowed internal accessory apartments, but backyard units were new.

How big can an American Fork detached ADU be?

The footprint cannot exceed 1,500 square feet or the footprint of the main home, whichever is smaller. The unit also cannot be taller than the main house or exceed 24 feet.

How close to the property line can it sit?

Most side and rear setbacks are at least 12 feet, rising to 20 feet on the street-facing side of a corner lot. The ADU and other accessory buildings together cannot cover more than 25 percent of the open space in the rear yard.

How much parking is required?

One on-site space for a unit smaller than 650 square feet, and two for a unit of 650 square feet or more, including exactly 650.

Do I have to live on the property?

Yes. The owner must live in either the main home or the ADU. Only one accessory dwelling unit is allowed per lot, and the backyard unit cannot be rented for fewer than 90 consecutive days.

Could American Fork change these rules?

Yes. The council passed the ordinance to meet the state deadline and said it would revisit parking, height and infrastructure and impact fees. Check with the city for the current text before you finalize a design.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with American Fork before you design or buy.
