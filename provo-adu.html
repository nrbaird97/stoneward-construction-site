Provo, Utah County

Provo is moving to open its backyards.

Provo's rules asked for at least four off-street parking spaces for a house and its ADU combined, which made detached units hard. Under SB 284 that has to change, and the city has a draft that allows detached ADUs on lots of 11,000 square feet or more.

About Provo

A college town where most households rent.

Provo is the Utah County seat and home to Brigham Young University, with Provo Canyon to the east and Utah Lake to the west. A 2023 city report found about 59 percent of Provo households rent, much higher than the state average, so rental demand is a bigger part of the conversation here than in most Utah cities.

Most of the lots that newly qualify are near the edges of town: along the foothills east and north of BYU, and on the west side toward Utah Lake, where parcels are larger and clear the quarter-acre mark. The parking change may matter more than the lot size. Going from four required spaces for house and ADU to one or two for the ADU alone could make a backyard unit work on lots where it never penciled out.

Questions

Provo ADU questions, answered.

Are detached ADUs allowed in Provo?

State law requires it on qualifying lots. Provo's Planning Commission recommended approval of its ordinance 6 to 0 on October 14, 2026, as reported, and a City Council vote was expected in November. Ask Provo Planning for the current status.

How much parking will Provo require?

Provo's previous rule asked for at least four off-street spaces for a house and ADU combined. State law caps parking for the detached unit at one space under 650 square feet or two for 650 square feet and larger.

How tall can a Provo detached ADU be?

The city's draft allowed newly eligible units up to 25 feet tall. The final number depends on the council.

Which Provo lots qualify?

Most newly qualifying lots are on the edges of the city, along the foothills east and north of BYU and on the west side toward Utah Lake, where parcels are larger.

Can I rent a Provo ADU to students?

State law lets a city prohibit rentals under 90 consecutive days and require the owner to live on the property, so a long-term rental may be possible. Check Provo's rules before you plan around it.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Provo before you design or buy.
