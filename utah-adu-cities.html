Utah ADU rules by city

Which cities now allow a backyard ADU?

Utah's SB 284 required larger cities to allow detached ADUs by October 1, 2026. Here is where the cities we work in stand, based on council records and local reporting.

By county

Utah County, Salt Lake County and Davis County.

Utah County

The biggest wave of change. American Fork, Orem, Saratoga Springs, Lehi, Mapleton, Highland, Provo, Alpine, Vineyard, Pleasant Grove, Lindon. Several never allowed backyard units before this fall, while Lehi simply lowered its lot-size minimum.

Salt Lake County

Sandy, Taylorsville, West Valley City. Larger cities with a wide range of lot sizes, so the lot-by-lot check matters most here, and utility costs can decide whether a project makes sense.

Davis County

Bountiful, West Point, Layton. Bountiful moved from conditional use to administrative approval, West Point is rewriting its section, and Layton already had a detached ADU ordinance.

Questions

Before you pick a city page.

Why do the rules differ from city to city?

SB 284 sets a floor: covered cities must permit detached ADUs on lots of 11,000 square feet or more. Within that, each city can set its own size, height, setback, owner-occupancy and rental rules, so two neighbors in different cities can get different answers.

How current is this information?

It reflects council records and news coverage through early November 2026. Several cities said they will revisit their rules, so confirm the current ordinance with your city before you design.
