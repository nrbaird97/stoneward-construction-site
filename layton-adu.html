Layton, Davis County

Layton already allows detached ADUs, and is updating the rules.

Unlike many Utah cities, Layton did not have to start from zero. It adopted standards for internal, attached and detached ADUs in 2021. The city is now amending its detached ADU standards to line up with SB 284, so the lot-size and size details are worth confirming.

About Layton

Davis County's biggest city, with a head start.

Layton is the largest city in Davis County, running from the Wasatch foothills on the east toward the Great Salt Lake on the west, with Hill Air Force Base along its southern edge. Its neighborhoods range from older streets near the center to newer subdivisions toward the west, and a steady local workforce tied to the base and to the Layton Hills area.

What sets Layton apart from the other cities we cover is that it already had a detached ADU ordinance. For a homeowner, that means the question is less "is it allowed" and more "do the current numbers fit my lot". SB 284 sets a ceiling on how much lot a city can demand, so Layton's amendment is about making sure its standards do not ask for more than state law allows.

Questions

Layton ADU questions, answered.

Does Layton allow detached ADUs?

Yes. Layton adopted Ordinance 21-23 on September 16, 2021, creating standards for internal, attached and detached accessory dwelling units. A permit is required, and the standards are in Section 19.06.300.

Is Layton changing its rules for SB 284?

A Planning Commission agenda proposed amending the detached ADU standards in Title 19. We could not confirm whether the council has adopted the amendment, so ask Layton Planning which version applies.

What lot size does Layton require?

State law says a covered city cannot require more than 11,000 square feet for a detached ADU on a lot with a single-family home. Layton's own figure should be confirmed with the city.

Do I need a permit for a detached ADU in Layton?

Yes. Layton's ADU handout says a permit is required.

How much parking will I need?

State law caps it at one on-site space under 650 square feet and two at 650 square feet or more.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Layton before you design or buy.
