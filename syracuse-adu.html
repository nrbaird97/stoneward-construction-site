Syracuse, Davis County

Syracuse follows state law: detached ADUs on nearly all residential lots.

On November 18, 2026 the Syracuse City Planner told us in writing that, per state code, detached ADUs are allowed on nearly all residential lots in the city, and that Syracuse observes the state statute by not requiring anything beyond it, including conditional use permits. The printed code (section 10.30.020(E)) has not been updated yet, so it still shows the older 10,000 square foot lot and conditional permit language.

About Syracuse

Flat Great Salt Lake lots, new subdivisions and a conditional permit.

Syracuse sits in northwest Davis County between Clearfield, Layton and West Point, on the flat ground west of the Wasatch Front toward the Great Salt Lake, and it is the city where the causeway to Antelope Island begins. Much of it was built out in newer subdivisions and planned communities, with level lots, wide streets and little of the foothill grade that complicates a delivery elsewhere in the county.

That mix shows up in the code. The city has a residential planned community zone, RPC, with lots as small as 5,600 square feet, and the council raised the ADU minimum there to 6,000 in 2025. The older printed code set 10,000 square feet outside RPC, but the city planner now says it follows the state law and allows detached ADUs on nearly all residential lots. The older code also listed a recorded owner-occupancy deed restriction and a ban on nightly or weekly rentals, so ask about those for your lot.

What it means for you

What this means for a Syracuse homeowner.

Nearly all residential lots

The planner says detached ADUs are allowed on nearly all residential lots, with exceptions such as RPC lots under 6,000 square feet. Ask the city to verify your lot.

Footprint no bigger than your house

The unit cannot exceed the footprint of the main home or 1,500 square feet of living area, whichever is less. A small rambler caps a small unit.

Parking tied to bedrooms

One added space for a studio or one bedroom, two for two or more bedrooms, and the unit parking must be independent of the main home spaces.

No conditional permit, code update pending

The city says it is not requiring a conditional use permit or anything beyond state law, even though the printed code is not yet updated.

Getting it built in Syracuse

From a Syracuse lot to move-in.

The full process
  1. Check the lot. Find your zone and ask the planner to verify it. In the RPC zone the lot must be at least 6,000 square feet.
  2. Get the standards. The planner says height and setbacks match the general accessory building standards. Ask the Planning Department for that table.
  3. Choose a plan. Keep the footprint under your main home footprint and match parking to the bedroom count.
  4. Permit and deed restriction. We prepare the plan set and site plan for the building permit, and we confirm with the city whether the older owner-occupancy deed restriction still applies.
  5. Site work, set and finish. Foundation and utilities, crane day, then finish work and final inspection before anyone moves in.

Questions

Syracuse ADU questions, answered.

Does Syracuse allow detached ADUs?

Yes. The Syracuse City Planner wrote to us on November 18, 2026 that, per state code, detached ADUs are allowed on nearly all residential lots, with exceptions such as RPC lots under 6,000 square feet. The printed code (section 10.30.020(E)) still needs to be updated.

What lot size does a detached ADU need in Syracuse?

The planner says nearly all residential lots qualify under state law. The older printed code says 10,000 square feet, and in the RPC zone an ADU is not allowed on a lot under 6,000 square feet. Ask the city to verify your lot.

How big can a detached ADU be?

Per the city planner, no larger than the footprint of the main house (not its total square footage) or 1,500 square feet, whichever is less.

How much parking does it need?

One added on-site, off-street space for a studio or one-bedroom unit and two for a unit with two or more bedrooms. The council amended this rule in Ordinance 25-30, adopted July 8, 2025, and the unit parking has to be independent of the main household parking.

Do I have to live on the property?

Yes. An owner must live in the main home or the ADU, and a deed restriction saying so is recorded before the building permit. A temporary absence of three years or less is allowed for things like military service, a job assignment or a sabbatical.

Can I rent it by the night?

No. Syracuse code says neither dwelling unit may be used for nightly or weekly rental, and the ADU cannot be sold separately from the main home.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Syracuse before you design or buy.
