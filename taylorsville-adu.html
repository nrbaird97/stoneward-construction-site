About Taylorsville

Established neighborhoods and a city looking ahead.

Taylorsville sits in the west-central Salt Lake Valley, with the Jordan River along its east side and West Valley City, Murray and West Jordan around it. Valley Regional Park and the city's long-standing neighborhoods give it an established feel, and most of its housing predates the state's current ADU push.

The October 7 council briefing shows a city that took this carefully before adopting. The ordinance was reorganized into shared standards plus separate sections for internal and detached units, with a required process for converting garages, sheds and barns, as staff described it then. Staff also spent time on money: they warned the council that utility hookups, not the building, could be the largest part of a detached unit's cost.

Questions

Taylorsville ADU questions, answered.

Are detached ADUs allowed in Taylorsville?

Yes, on qualifying lots. They were prohibited, according to the city's own notice, but the City Council adopted Ordinance 26-15 on October 21, 2026, by a 5 to 0 vote, allowing detached ADUs on residential lots of 11,000 square feet or more. Confirm setbacks and height with Taylorsville Planning before you design.

How much do utilities add to a detached ADU?

Taylorsville staff told the council that utility connection costs could run from 50 to 70 percent of the cost of a detached unit. It varies a lot by lot, depending on how far sewer, water and power are from the new unit. We price the site work line by line before you commit.

Can I convert my garage or shed in Taylorsville?

State law requires a conversion process for legally built accessory structures, and Taylorsville staff noted it would apply to garages, sheds and barns. The structure still has to meet setbacks and building, health and fire codes.

Can I rent a Taylorsville ADU short term?

Taylorsville staff noted an inconsistency in state law: short-term rentals are defined as fewer than 30 consecutive days for internal units but fewer than 90 days for detached units. Ask the city how it will apply the rule.

When did the rules take effect?

The council adopted the ordinance on October 21, 2026. State law, effective October 1, 2026, already required it. We have asked the city for the signed text and will update this page with setbacks and height details.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Taylorsville before you design or buy.
