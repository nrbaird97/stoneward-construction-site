Saratoga Springs, Utah County

Saratoga Springs has opened the door to backyard ADUs.

The Saratoga Springs City Council adopted its detached ADU rules on September 15, 2026, to comply with SB 284. Owners of qualifying lots can now plan a separate small home in the backyard, with local standards the city says it will keep refining.

About Saratoga Springs

A fast-growing lakeside city with a lot of HOAs.

Saratoga Springs spreads along the northwest shore of Utah Lake, with the Lake Mountains to the west and Lehi to the north. The city grew quickly from a small lakeside community into one of Utah County's larger cities, and much of its housing is in newer subdivisions built over the last two decades.

Two things make Saratoga Springs different from the other cities we cover. First, many neighborhoods have homeowners associations, and at the September 15 meeting the city attorney said the city does not enforce HOA covenants. Those are separate contracts, so your CC&Rs can still say no. Second, the council flagged questions about emergency access and utility capacity, so lot-by-lot details matter here.

Questions

Saratoga Springs ADU questions, answered.

When did Saratoga Springs adopt its detached ADU rules?

The City Council adopted Ordinance 26-42 on September 15, 2026, by a 4 to 0 vote, according to the meeting minutes. It amends Chapter 19.20 of the Land Development Code to cover internal and detached accessory dwelling units.

Will Saratoga Springs charge impact fees on a detached ADU?

The city said it may impose impact fees on detached ADUs, though not on internal ones. Ask the city for the current fee schedule early, because it affects your budget.

Does my HOA still apply?

Yes, possibly. The city attorney said Saratoga Springs does not involve itself in HOA rules or covenants, which are contracts between the HOA and homeowners, though state law may supersede them. Read your CC&Rs and ask your HOA.

Is there a maximum size for a Saratoga Springs ADU?

The council discussed a maximum size, but we could not confirm the adopted figure. State law lets cities limit overall size, so confirm the number with the city before you pick a plan.

Will the rules change again?

Likely. The mayor said there will be more work on it in the next few months, and the city manager called this the first step to meet state requirements.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Saratoga Springs before you design or buy.
