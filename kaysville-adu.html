Kaysville, Davis County

Kaysville now allows backyard ADUs on larger lots.

On September 17, 2026 the Kaysville City Council adopted Ordinance 26-09-04, rewriting the city's accessory dwelling unit section to allow detached units. The rules are specific, and a few of them, like where the unit can sit and how it is rented, shape which plan will work.

Adopted Sep 17, 2026

What we confirmed for Kaysville

Source: Kaysville City Ordinance 26-09-04, adopted September 17, 2026, as posted on the Utah Public Notice Website on September 29, 2026; Utah Code 10-21-304.

Official text: Ordinance 26-09-04 (Utah Public Notice Website) Notice of adoption

Lot
A detached ADU is allowed on a lot of 11,000 sq ft or more with a single-family detached home
Size
No larger than 50 percent of the main home footprint (garage included) or 1,200 sq ft, whichever is smaller
Height
The height of the main home or 25 ft to the roof peak, whichever is less
Location
Not in the front yard, and not inside a public utility or other recorded easement
Setbacks
Single story: the rear yard setback of the main home on the rear, with the side yard increased to match. Two story: the main home side and rear setbacks
Parking
1 off-street space at 650 sq ft or less, 2 above 650 sq ft, paved and not in tandem with the main home spaces
Occupancy
Owner lives in the main home or the ADU, with a signed owner-occupancy affidavit. One ADU per lot
Rentals
Minimum 90 consecutive days, with a city permit and rental license first
Utilities
No separate utility meters, and not allowed on a property with a failing septic tank

Not confirmed: The underlying zone setback numbers, which the ordinance points to rather than lists, and any change made after the September 29 posting. Ask Kaysville Community Development for the setback table for your zone.

About Kaysville

Old streets, big lots and a council that chose strict standards.

Kaysville sits in central Davis County between Farmington and Layton, with the Wasatch foothills behind it and Antelope Island and the Great Salt Lake to the west. Its older neighborhoods around Main Street and Center Street have deep lots, and many of those lots carry barns, shops or a detached garage that has stood for decades.

The council wrote one of the more detailed ordinances on the Wasatch Front. It ties the unit's size to the main home, bars a second meter, and requires paved, non-tandem parking. It also asks that a detached unit be designed consistently with the main house. That detail is why a plan should be chosen against the ordinance, not against the 11,000 square foot rule alone.

What it means for you

What this means for a Kaysville homeowner.

Size follows your house

The cap is half of the main home footprint with the garage counted, or 1,200 square feet. A small rambler with a one-car garage may land well under 1,200.

Rentals of 90 days or longer

Kaysville requires a permit and a city rental license before the unit can be rented, and no stay under 90 consecutive days.

One utility connection

The ordinance says an ADU cannot run on separate utility meters, so plan for shared service and a clear arrangement with any occupant.

Not in a front yard or easement

The unit must sit behind the main home and clear of any recorded easement, so the survey matters before the design.

Questions

Kaysville ADU questions, answered.

Did Kaysville adopt detached ADU rules?

Yes. The City Council approved Ordinance 26-09-04 on September 17, 2026, repealing and readopting section 17-31-3 of the city code to allow detached accessory dwelling units. The notice was posted on September 29, 2026.

What lot size does Kaysville require?

11,000 square feet or larger, with a single-family detached home that is a permitted use on the lot. The state minimum ceiling is the same number.

How big can a detached ADU be in Kaysville?

No more than 50 percent of the main home footprint, garage included, or 1,200 square feet, whichever is smaller. A two-story unit with a garage on the first floor is measured by its living area.

Do I have to live on the property?

Yes. The owner must live in the main home or the ADU as a primary residence and sign an owner-occupancy affidavit when applying.

Can I rent a Kaysville ADU short term?

No. A detached unit cannot be rented for less than 90 consecutive days, and the owner must hold the permit and a city rental license first.

How much parking does it need?

One off-street space for a unit of 650 square feet or less, and two above 650, paved and not in tandem with the spaces for the main home.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Kaysville before you design or buy.
