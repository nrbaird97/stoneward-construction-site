About Sandy

A south-valley city under the Wasatch with a split vote.

Sandy sits at the south end of the Salt Lake Valley, up against the Wasatch Front and the mouths of Big and Little Cottonwood canyons, with Draper, Midvale and Cottonwood Heights as neighbors. Its housing runs from older neighborhoods near the valley floor to newer and larger-lot areas toward the east bench.

Sandy's council was divided. The ordinance passed 4 to 2 after members debated lot size, height and the size of the unit relative to the main house. The reported 18 foot height cap is the number that shapes your choice of plan, and the lot-size threshold was the most contested point, with the planning commission discussing a lower figure than the state's 11,000 square feet.

What it means for you

What this means for a Sandy homeowner.

A split council

The 4 to 2 vote means the rules may be revisited. Ask for the current text before you design.

Low height cap

About 1.5 stories or 18 feet favors single-level and low-profile plans.

A size cap, too

A reported 2,000 square foot cap on gross area is far above what most of our ADU plans need.

Salt Lake County market

Sandy is one of the larger Salt Lake County cities covered by SB 284, and one where lot size varies a lot from block to block.

Questions

Sandy ADU questions, answered.

Has Sandy adopted a detached ADU ordinance?

Yes. Sandy adopted Ordinance 26-55 this fall, by a reported 4 to 2 vote, and Deseret News lists Sandy among cities adopting ordinances that comply with the state law.

How tall can a detached ADU be in Sandy?

The council reportedly capped height at about 1.5 stories, around 18 feet. Confirm the exact figure with Sandy Planning before you choose a plan.

What is the minimum lot size in Sandy?

State law sets 11,000 square feet as the largest minimum a city can require. The planning commission discussed a lower threshold, and we could not confirm the final number, so check with the city.

Is there a size cap?

The council reportedly added a 2,000 square foot cap on gross area. Our ADU plans are far smaller.

Does Sandy require owner occupancy?

The adopted ordinance includes owner-occupancy and subordinate-unit provisions, according to local coverage.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Sandy before you design or buy.
