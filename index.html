Why a modular ADU

A calmer way to add a home.

Stick-built can work. It also puts every trade, permit and weather day on your lot. A modular build moves the house into a factory and keeps the site work moving alongside it.

The crew backlog

A site build waits its turn for framers, electricians and plumbers. With modular, much of the construction happens inside a factory, where the work is part of a production schedule.

Cost creep on stick-built

When a home is built in the open, changes and surprises can add up. A factory plan and a written scope give you a clearer picture before construction starts. Your quote confirms pricing and delivery.

Weather delays

Rain, wind and cold can stop work on an exposed site. Modules are built indoors. Site work still follows the weather, but the house itself is not sitting in it.

Managing permits and trades

A modular ADU still needs permits, inspections and site work. Stoneward coordinates the local pieces and works directly with the factory, so you are not refereeing separate companies.

Common questions

What homeowners ask first.

All questions and answers
Is a modular ADU really faster?

Usually, yes. The home is built in the factory at the same time the foundation and site work happen on your lot, instead of one trade waiting on the next. Our factory cites industry research showing 40 to 70 percent less time on site.

A shorter build on your lot also means less disruption for you and your neighbors, and less exposure to weather, theft and vandalism. Permits and site conditions still set the pace, and we'll be straight with you about both.

Does it cost less than building on site?

Sometimes. It is not a guarantee. Where modular tends to save money is in factory labor efficiency, less material waste, shorter financing and carrying costs, and fewer change orders, because the full design is finalized before the build starts.

Your written quote is what confirms the number for your project.

Is the quality as good as a site-built home?

It is built to the same state and local residential code as a house built on your lot. It is not a mobile or manufactured home, which is built to a different federal code.

The home is framed indoors on a flat factory floor, so materials are not left out in rain, wind and sun. Our factory runs 551 quality control points on each project, and modules are reinforced to handle transport and craning.

What are the steps from start to finish?

A feasibility review of your lot, then preconstruction where design and finishes are settled. After a site visit and approvals, the home is built in the factory, delivered, set on your foundation by crane and finished on site.

See the full process and milestones

Can I see my home while it is being built?

Yes. There is a required walkthrough before insulation and drywall go in, and another before the home is wrapped for shipping. Both can be done in person or virtually.

What does the home price leave out?

The factory price covers the home, its listed features, engineering and state approvals. Site work, the foundation, utility connections, the on-site finish work that joins the modules, and local permits and fees are budgeted separately. We help you put numbers to each line before you commit.
