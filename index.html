Why a modular ADU

A calmer way to add a home.

Stick-built can work. It also puts every trade, permit and weather day on your lot. A modular build moves the house into a factory and keeps the site work moving alongside it.

The crew backlog

A site build waits its turn for framers, electricians and roofers. With modular, much of the construction happens inside a factory, where the work is part of a production schedule.

Cost creep on stick-built

When a home is built in the open, changes and surprises can add up. A factory plan and a written scope give you a clearer picture before construction starts. Your quote confirms pricing and delivery.

Weather delays

Rain, wind and cold can stop work on an exposed site. Modules are built indoors. Site work still follows the weather, but the house itself is not sitting in it.

Managing permits and trades

A modular ADU still needs permits, inspections and site work. Stoneward coordinates the local pieces and works with Irontown's process, so you are not refereeing separate companies.

Modular vs stick-built

Two ways to build. Different trade-offs.

Both paths can produce a good home. Here is the practical difference, in plain terms.

Modular ADU Stick-built on site

Design

Modular ADU

A factory plan set with finish selections.

Stick-built

Drawn for your lot and built as work progresses.

Price certainty

Modular ADU

A written quote before construction starts.

Stick-built

Selections, site conditions and changes can move the number.

Carrying costs

Modular ADU

Site work and factory work can run alongside each other.

Stick-built

Trades are sequenced on site, one after another.

Weather

Modular ADU

The house is built indoors. Site work still follows the weather.

Stick-built

Every stage of the house is exposed to the weather.

Every lot and project is different. These are general differences, not a promise about your project. Your approved plans and written quote govern the work.
