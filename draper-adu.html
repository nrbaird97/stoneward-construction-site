Draper, Salt Lake County

Draper already allows detached ADUs, with its own limits.

Draper has had a detached ADU (D-ADU) program with a permit for years. The city's own ADU page lists a 12,000 square foot lot minimum, while Utah's new law sets 11,000 for covered cities as of October 1, 2026. We have not seen a Draper update to that number, so treat the city figure as a starting point.

About Draper

Foothill lots, large yards and a permit that comes first.

Draper sits at the south end of Salt Lake County, with the Wasatch foothills and Corner Canyon on the east, the Traverse Mountains to the south and Sandy to the north. Many of its neighborhoods have larger lots than the rest of the valley, which is part of why a 12,000 square foot lot minimum is workable here.

What stands out in Draper's rules is the size cap. Instead of a fixed number of square feet, a detached ADU can be no more than half the square footage of the main home, so the same lot can allow a very different unit depending on the house in front. Draper also requires an ADU permit before the unit can be rented or even offered for rent, and it asks that the unit match the main home in color, materials and design.

What it means for you

What this means for a Draper homeowner.

Half the main home

The D-ADU is capped at 50 percent of the main home's square footage, so check your house size first.

Permit before renting

A valid ADU permit is required before the unit can be rented, leased or offered for rent.

Match the house

The D-ADU must match the main home architecturally in color, materials and design.

One extra space

At least one additional on-site parking space is required, not tandem and not directly behind another space.

Hillside lots and the crane path.

Draper lots near the foothills can slope, have long driveways or sit behind mature landscaping, and a modular home is set by crane. Questions

Draper ADU questions, answered.

Does Draper allow detached ADUs?

Yes. Draper's city page says a detached ADU is allowed on residentially zoned lots of at least 12,000 square feet, with an ADU permit. Utah's new law sets 11,000 square feet for covered cities as of October 1, 2026, so confirm the current number with Draper Planning.

How big can a Draper detached ADU be?

No more than 50 percent of the main home's square footage, and the building height is limited to 35 feet, according to the city page.

Do I have to live on the property?

Yes. The main home must be owner occupied, and only one detached ADU is allowed per property.

How long must a tenant stay?

At least 30 consecutive days. Draper's page says the city has no short-term rental rules specific to ADUs, but ADUs must be rented for 30 days or longer.

What parking is required?

At least one extra on-site parking space, not tandem and not directly behind another space. State law also limits how much parking a city can require.

Can I use a separate utility meter?

No. The city page says a D-ADU may not be served by separate utility meters from the main home.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Draper before you design or buy.
