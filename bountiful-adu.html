About Bountiful

Bench-area lots and a one-step process.

Bountiful sits in Davis County between Salt Lake City and Farmington, with older neighborhoods on the benches below the Wasatch Front and newer streets toward the valley. Mueller Park and the mountain trails are close, and many homes have mature yards and some slope.

Bountiful is a rare example of a city that already allowed detached ADUs and still made a meaningful change. Before, each unit needed a conditional use permit, which meant a discretionary hearing. Ordinance 2026-08 replaces that with an administrative approval and objective standards. If you already knew you qualified, the new path is simpler, faster and more predictable.

Questions

Bountiful ADU questions, answered.

What changed in Bountiful?

Bountiful's code treated detached ADUs as conditional uses. On September 22, 2026 the City Council adopted Ordinance 2026-08, which establishes an administrative approval process and objective development standards.

Do I still need a hearing?

For a qualifying detached ADU, approval is now administrative. You still need a building permit and plan review.

What are Bountiful's size and height standards?

The ordinance sets standards for lot size, unit size, owner occupancy, parking, height and setbacks. We could not confirm the specific figures, so confirm them with Bountiful Planning.

Did the Planning Commission support the change?

Yes. The Planning Commission held a public hearing on September 15, 2026 and recommended approval 7 to 0 before the City Council adopted the ordinance.

Can I build on a sloped Bountiful lot?

Often, but a slope can add grading, drainage and retaining costs and limit crane access. We review it in a lot visit.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Bountiful before you design or buy.
