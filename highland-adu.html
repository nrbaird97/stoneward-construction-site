About Highland

Foothill lots, long driveways and a title-recorded rule.

Highland sits on the east side of Utah Valley between American Fork and Alpine, along the foothills with mountain views and the Alpine Highway running through it. Many residential lots here can be larger than the quarter-acre state threshold, so the rule may reach a good share of homeowners.

Highland's ordinance is notable for one specific rule: owner occupancy is recorded against the property title. That makes the occupancy requirement a lasting condition on the property, not a promise you make at the permit counter. It is worth understanding before you build, especially if you ever plan to sell or hand the property to family.

What it means for you

What this means for a Highland homeowner.

One ADU per property

Highland allows one accessory dwelling per property, internal or detached, so an ADU in the house means none in the yard.

Owner occupancy on title

The requirement is recorded against your title, so it follows the property.

Building and fire codes apply

The city requires compliance with building and fire codes, which a factory-built unit is designed to meet.

Separate detached rules

Detached units have their own size, setback and height limits, so lot shape matters.

Questions

Highland ADU questions, answered.

When did Highland adopt its detached ADU rules?

The City Council adopted Ordinance O-2026-28 on September 1, 2026, according to the city's legal notice. Local coverage reported a 5 to 0 vote.

Do I have to live on a Highland property with an ADU?

Yes. Owner occupancy is required and is recorded against the property title.

Can I have two ADUs in Highland?

No. The ordinance allows one ADU per property, whether internal or detached.

What size can a Highland detached ADU be?

Detached ADUs have their own size, setback and height limits. The planning commission proposed living-area caps in July, but we could not confirm the adopted figures. Check with Highland Planning.

Do building and fire codes apply?

Yes. The ordinance requires compliance with building and fire codes. A modular unit is built to the residential code and inspected during the factory build.

General information, not legal advice. Cities can amend their ordinances, so confirm current rules with Highland before you design or buy.
